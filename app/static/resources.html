Kueue · LocalQueue

training-queue

k8s/02-kueue-config.yaml · kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
Why it matters

ML researchers and application teams never touch cluster-level GPU pools or node selectors directly. They submit standard Kubernetes Jobs to their namespace's training-queue, giving platform admins clean multi-tenant governance while keeping developer workflows simple.

metadata.namespace: default Namespace-scoped queue entry point. Different teams can each get their own LocalQueue pointing to a shared ClusterQueue.
spec.clusterQueue: gpu-cluster-queue Forwards submitted jobs into the cluster-wide quota pool that manages the 4 time-shared GPU seats.
LocalQueue manifest 
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: LocalQueue
metadata:
  name: training-queue
  namespace: default
spec:
  clusterQueue: gpu-cluster-queue
Kueue · ClusterQueue

gpu-cluster-queue

k8s/02-kueue-config.yaml · kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
Why it matters

gpu-cluster-queue is the brain of our scheduling policy. It caps concurrent admissions at 4 GPU slices (matching 1 physical GPU with 4× time-sharing) and sets preemption: Never—so a running research job is never killed mid-epoch, zero GPU compute is wasted, and urgent jobs cleanly take the very next freed seat.

spec.preemption.withinClusterQueue: Never Guarantees non-preemptive scheduling. Running training runs finish naturally instead of losing uncheckpointed epochs.
spec.queueingStrategy: BestEffortFIFO Orders waiting jobs by WorkloadPriorityClass first, then arrival timestamp, without blocking smaller jobs if quota is available.
nominalQuota: nvidia.com/gpu: 4 (16 cpu · 64Gi) Matches the 4 time-shared GPU seats advertised by 1 physical L4 or T4 GPU node. Once 4 jobs run, job #5 waits in Kueue.
ClusterQueue manifest 
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: ClusterQueue
metadata:
  name: gpu-cluster-queue
spec:
  namespaceSelector: {}
  queueingStrategy: BestEffortFIFO
  preemption:
    withinClusterQueue: Never
    reclaimWithinCohort: Never
  resourceGroups:
    - coveredResources: ["cpu", "memory", "nvidia.com/gpu"]
      flavors:
        - name: cheap-gpu-flavor
          resources:
            - name: "cpu"
              nominalQuota: 16
            - name: "memory"
              nominalQuota: 64Gi
            - name: "nvidia.com/gpu"
              nominalQuota: 4
Kueue · WorkloadPriorityClass

critical-priority (1000) & low-priority (100)

k8s/02-kueue-config.yaml · kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
Why it matters

Why use Kueue's WorkloadPriorityClass instead of standard Kubernetes Pod PriorityClass? Standard Pod PriorityClass triggers Kubelet pod eviction on nodes. Kueue's WorkloadPriorityClass controls queue order only—so urgent production retraining (1000) jumps straight to #1 in the waiting line ahead of research jobs (100) without evicting running pods!

critical-priority → value: 1000 Assigned to urgent production retraining jobs via label kueue.x-k8s.io/priority-class: critical-priority.
low-priority → value: 100 Assigned to batch hyperparameter sweeps and research jobs so they fill spare GPU seats opportunistically.
WorkloadPriorityClass manifests 
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: WorkloadPriorityClass
metadata:
  name: critical-priority
value: 1000
description: "Critical production small-model retraining. Jumps to the front of the Kueue waiting queue."
---
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: WorkloadPriorityClass
metadata:
  name: low-priority
value: 100
description: "Low-priority batch hyperparameter sweeps and experimental small-model training."
Kueue · ResourceFlavor

cheap-gpu-flavor

k8s/02-kueue-config.yaml · kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
Why it matters

ResourceFlavor bridges Kueue's quota accounting with GKE's hardware provisioning. When Kueue admits a job from gpu-cluster-queue, this flavor automatically stamps the pod with the cheap-gpu-timeshared node label and GPU taint tolerations so GKE provisions the exact right GPU machine.

spec.nodeLabels["cloud.google.com/compute-class"] Binds admitted pods to GKE's cheap-gpu-timeshared Custom ComputeClass.
spec.tolerations (compute-class & nvidia.com/gpu) Lets admitted GPU jobs schedule onto tainted GPU nodes while preventing non-GPU workloads from landing on GPU VMs.
ResourceFlavor manifest 
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: ResourceFlavor
metadata:
  name: cheap-gpu-flavor
spec:
  nodeLabels:
    cloud.google.com/compute-class: cheap-gpu-timeshared
  tolerations:
    - key: cloud.google.com/compute-class
      operator: Equal
      value: cheap-gpu-timeshared
      effect: NoSchedule
    - key: nvidia.com/gpu
      operator: Exists
      effect: NoSchedule
GKE · ComputeClass

cheap-gpu-timeshared

k8s/01-compute-class.yaml · cloud.google.com/v1
Why it matters

Instead of hardcoding one GPU SKU and getting stuck during regional stockouts in Singapore (asia-southeast1), GKE's ComputeClass evaluates On-Demand NVIDIA L4 and T4 together at priorityScore: 20, automatically falls back to Spot L4/T4 at priorityScore: 10, and enables 4× GPU Time-Sharing so 4 small models share 1 cheap physical GPU!

nodePoolAutoCreation.enabled: true GKE automatically creates and scales node pools matching these rules, and scales idle GPU nodes back to zero.
priorityScore: 20 (spot: false) → priorityScore: 10 (spot: true) Grouping L4 and T4 at the same score lets GKE pick whichever GPU has immediate capacity in the region before falling back to Spot.
gpuSharing: TIME_SHARING (maxSharedClientsPerGPU: 4) Makes the node advertise 4 nvidia.com/gpu allocatable units so 4 small PyTorch jobs (~25% load each) drive utilization near 100%.
ComputeClass manifest 
apiVersion: cloud.google.com/v1
kind: ComputeClass
metadata:
  name: cheap-gpu-timeshared
spec:
  nodePoolAutoCreation:
    enabled: true
  whenUnsatisfiable: DoNotScaleUp
  priorities:
    # Tier 1: On-Demand L4 & T4 evaluated together (score 20)
    - gpu:
        type: nvidia-l4
        count: 1
        driverVersion: default
        gpuSharing:
          sharingStrategy: TIME_SHARING
          maxSharedClientsPerGPU: 4
      spot: false
      priorityScore: 20
    - gpu:
        type: nvidia-tesla-t4
        count: 1
        driverVersion: default
        gpuSharing:
          sharingStrategy: TIME_SHARING
          maxSharedClientsPerGPU: 4
      spot: false
      priorityScore: 20
    # Tier 2: Spot L4 & T4 fallback (score 10)
    - gpu:
        type: nvidia-l4
        count: 1
        driverVersion: default
        gpuSharing:
          sharingStrategy: TIME_SHARING
          maxSharedClientsPerGPU: 4
      spot: true
      priorityScore: 10
    - gpu:
        type: nvidia-tesla-t4
        count: 1
        driverVersion: default
        gpuSharing:
          sharingStrategy: TIME_SHARING
          maxSharedClientsPerGPU: 4
      spot: true
      priorityScore: 10
batch/v1 · Job

critical-retrain-* & batch-sweep-*

k8s/03-job-templates.yaml · batch/v1
Why it matters

Adopting Kueue requires almost zero changes to existing training code: it is a normal Kubernetes batch/v1 Job with suspend: true and two labels. Kueue keeps the job suspended while queued, then flips suspend: false the instant a GPU slice frees up.

spec.suspend: true Tells Kubernetes not to create pods immediately—Kueue unsuspends the Job only after admitting it against ClusterQueue quota.
labels["kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name" & "priority-class"] Routes the job to training-queue and tags it as critical-priority (1000) or low-priority (100).
resources.requests["nvidia.com/gpu"]: "1" Requests 1 of the 4 time-shared GPU seats on the node; Kubernetes still enforces full container CPU/memory isolation.
Urgent Job template (excerpt) 
apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: Job
metadata:
  generateName: critical-retrain-
  namespace: default
  labels:
    kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name: training-queue
    kueue.x-k8s.io/priority-class: critical-priority
spec:
  suspend: true
  parallelism: 1
  completions: 1
  template:
    spec:
      restartPolicy: Never
      nodeSelector:
        cloud.google.com/compute-class: cheap-gpu-timeshared
      containers:
        - name: trainer
          image: pytorch/pytorch:2.5.1-cuda12.4-cudnn9-runtime
          resources:
            requests:
              cpu: "500m"
              memory: "2Gi"
              nvidia.com/gpu: "1"
            limits:
              cpu: "1"
              memory: "4Gi"
              nvidia.com/gpu: "1"

Useful kubectl commands for live cluster inspection

To inspect Kueue or GKE objects directly from the terminal, copy any command below.

Inspect ClusterQueue quota & pending workloads kubectl get clusterqueue gpu-cluster-queue -o wide
Watch Kueue Workloads queue order & priority live kubectl get workloads -n default -w
Verify GKE ComputeClass L4/T4 rules & time-sharing kubectl describe computeclass cheap-gpu-timeshared
Confirm 1 physical GPU exposes 4 allocatable GPU slices kubectl get nodes -l cloud.google.com/compute-class=cheap-gpu-timeshared -o custom-columns=NODE:.metadata.name,GPU_SLICES:.status.allocatable.nvidia\.com/gpu
